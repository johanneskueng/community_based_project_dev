# Youth Focus Group - Education Barriers

## Context
Focus group with youth aged 13-17 to discuss barriers to secondary education access. This session built on the initial community meeting and went deeper into youth-specific issues.

## Session Structure
1. **Icebreaker Activity** (15:00-15:15) - "Two Truths and a Dream"
2. **Barriers Discussion** (15:15-16:00) - Small group discussions
3. **Solutions Brainstorm** (16:00-16:45) - Group activity
4. **Wrap-up** (16:45-17:00) - Summary and next steps

## Key Findings

### Main Barriers Identified

#### 1. Distance and Transportation
- **Average Distance**: 7km to nearest secondary school
- **Travel Time**: 1.5-2 hours each way
- **Transportation Options**:
  - Walking: Free but tiring, especially in heat
  - Bicycle: Some have bikes, but road conditions are poor
  - Shared cars: Unreliable, often overcrowded
  - Motorcycle taxis: Expensive (~$2-3 per trip)
- **Impact on Attendance**: Many youth miss school during rainy season when roads are bad

#### 2. Cost
- **Direct Costs**:
  - Uniforms: $40-50 per year
  - School supplies: $25-30 per term
  - Examination fees: $15-20 per year
- **Indirect Costs**:
  - Transportation: $10-15 per week
  - Lost income: Youth could be working instead of in school
  - Opportunity cost: Time spent traveling could be used for study or work
- **Family Priorities**: When resources are limited, boys are often prioritized over girls

#### 3. Time Conflicts
- **Household Chores**: Especially for girls, expected to help with cooking, cleaning, caring for younger siblings
- **Farm Work**: During planting and harvest seasons, youth are needed on the farm
- **Part-time Jobs**: Some youth work to contribute to family income
- **Marriage**: Early marriage is a concern, especially for girls

#### 4. School Environment
- **Facilities**: Limited resources (no library, few computers, poor lab equipment)
- **Teacher Quality**: Some teachers are good, others are frequently absent
- **Bullying**: Some youth report bullying, especially those from poorer families
- **Language**: Some struggle with English (language of instruction)

#### 5. Personal Factors
- **Health**: Some youth have health issues that affect attendance
- **Disability**: Limited accommodations for youth with disabilities
- **Motivation**: Some lack motivation, especially if they've fallen behind
- **Peer Pressure**: Negative peer influence can discourage school attendance

### Youth-Specific Solutions

#### Transportation Solutions
1. **Community Bus**: Organize a dedicated community bus
   - Pros: Reliable, safe, can be subsidized
   - Cons: High initial cost, maintenance

2. **Bicycle Program**: Provide bicycles to youth
   - Pros: Empowering, sustainable, good exercise
   - Cons: Road conditions, safety concerns

3. **Walking Groups**: Organize groups to walk together
   - Pros: Free, builds community, safer
   - Cons: Still long distance, weather-dependent

4. **Boarding at School**: Allow youth to board at school during week
   - Pros: Solves distance issue completely
   - Cons: Cost, separation from family

#### Cost Solutions
1. **Scholarship Fund**: Community fund to support families in need
2. **Uniform Exchange**: Program to reuse uniforms from graduated students
3. **Supply Sharing**: Community pool of school supplies
4. **Income Generation**: Projects to generate funds for education

#### Time Management Solutions
1. **Flexible Scheduling**: Work with school to accommodate community needs
2. **Study Groups**: Community-based study groups to catch up on missed work
3. **Chore Sharing**: Community systems to share household responsibilities
4. **Awareness Campaigns**: Educate families on the importance of education

## Youth Perspectives

### What Youth Want
- "I want to finish school so I can get a good job and help my family." - Carlos
- "I want to be a doctor, but I need to pass my exams first." - Priya
- "I want to learn computer skills so I can work in an office." - Jamal
- "I want to be the first in my family to finish secondary school." - Fatima

### What Youth Need
- Reliable transportation
- Financial support for school costs
- Support from their families
- Quality teachers and resources
- Safe and supportive school environment

### What Youth Can Contribute
- Time and energy to study
- Ideas and perspectives on solutions
- Peer support and mentorship
- Community service in exchange for support

## Gender-Specific Insights

### Girls
- Face additional barriers related to household chores and early marriage
- More likely to be kept home for safety concerns
- Often have less decision-making power in the family
- But also highly motivated and perform well when given the opportunity

### Boys
- More likely to be allowed to attend school
- Face pressure to contribute to family income
- More likely to be involved in risky behaviors (drugs, gangs)
- Need positive male role models

## Age-Specific Insights

### Younger Youth (13-14)
- More optimistic about education
- More dependent on family decisions
- Need more support and guidance

### Older Youth (15-17)
- More aware of the challenges
- More independent in decision-making
- More likely to have dropped out already
- Need flexible options to return to school

## Observations

### Positive Observations
- Youth are highly motivated and have clear goals
- Strong peer support among youth
- Youth have practical ideas for solutions
- Youth are willing to contribute to solutions (e.g., through community service)

### Concerns
- Some youth have already given up on education
- Gender disparities are significant
- Some youth lack family support
- Economic pressures are intense

### Surprising Insights
- Youth are more aware of their rights than expected
- Youth have strong networks and support each other
- Youth are willing to take on significant responsibility
- Some youth are already working on solutions informally
