# Parents Focus Group - Education Support

## Context
Focus group with parents to discuss their perspectives on education, the challenges they face in supporting their children's education, and their ideas for solutions. This session complemented the youth focus group by providing the parent perspective.

## Session Structure
1. **Introduction** (10:00-10:15) - Project overview and consent
2. **Parents' Education Experiences** (10:15-10:45) - Their own education and aspirations for their children
3. **Current Challenges** (10:45-11:15) - Barriers they face in supporting education
4. **Solutions Discussion** (11:15-11:45) - Parents' ideas for addressing challenges
5. **Wrap-up** (11:45-12:00) - Summary and next steps

### Parents' Aspirations for Their Children

#### Common Aspirations
- All parents want their children to complete at least secondary education
- Most want their children to go to university or vocational training
- Parents want their children to have better opportunities than they had
- There's a strong desire for children to "break the cycle of poverty"

#### Gender Differences in Aspirations
- **For Sons**: Professional careers (doctor, engineer, teacher, business owner)
- **For Daughters**: Professional careers (nurse, teacher, lawyer) or stable jobs
- **Note**: While aspirations are high for both genders, there's more acceptance of girls getting married young if education isn't possible

### Challenges Parents Face

#### Financial Challenges
- **School Costs**: Even "free" public school has many hidden costs
  - Uniforms: $40-50 per child per year
  - School supplies: $25-30 per term per child
  - Examination fees: $15-20 per child per year
  - **Total per child**: ~$100-150 per year
- **Multiple Children**: Families with 3-4 children in school face costs of $300-600 per year
- **Income Levels**: Most families earn $200-400 per month
- **Competing Priorities**: Food, housing, healthcare often take priority over education

#### Time Challenges
- **Household Responsibilities**: Especially for girls, expected to help with:
  - Cooking
  - Cleaning
  - Caring for younger siblings
  - Fetching water
  - Farm work
- **Work Demands**: Parents often need children to help with:
  - Farm work (especially during planting/harvest)
  - Family business
  - Household chores
- **Seasonal Variations**: More pressure during planting and harvest seasons

#### Social Challenges
- **Gender Norms**: Strong expectations that girls should help at home
- **Early Marriage**: Pressure for girls to marry young, especially if family can't afford education
- **Peer Pressure**: Youth face pressure from peers to skip school
- **Community Expectations**: Fear of judgment if children don't conform to norms

#### Infrastructure Challenges
- **Distance**: 7km to school is too far for many children to walk daily
- **Safety**: Concerns about children's safety, especially girls
- **Transportation**: No reliable, affordable transportation options
- **School Quality**: Concerns about the quality of education at the nearest school

### Parents' Coping Strategies

#### Current Strategies
1. **Prioritization**: Send some children to school, keep others at home
   - Often boys are prioritized over girls
   - Older children may be kept home to help with younger siblings

2. **Cost-Sharing**: Pool resources with other families
   - Share transportation costs
   - Share school supplies
   - Take turns hosting study groups

3. **Income Generation**: Find ways to generate additional income
   - Small businesses
   - Farming
   - Seasonal work

4. **Negotiation**: Work with schools for flexibility
   - Payment plans for fees
   - Reduced fees for multiple children
   - Scholarships and bursaries

### Parents' Ideas for Solutions

#### Financial Support
1. **Community Scholarship Fund**
   - Parents contribute what they can
   - Fund supports families in most need
   - Managed transparently by community committee

2. **Uniform and Supply Exchange**
   - Collect used uniforms and supplies from graduated students
   - Distribute to families in need
   - Organize at the start of each school year

3. **Income Generating Activities**
   - Community garden to generate funds
   - Small business cooperatives
   - Skills training for parents

#### Transportation Solutions
1. **Community Transportation Cooperative**
   - Pool resources to buy a vehicle
   - Hire a driver
   - Set a reasonable fare
   - Subsidize for families in need

2. **Bicycle Program**
   - Provide bicycles to students
   - Include safety training
   - Maintenance support

3. **Walking Groups**
   - Organize groups of students to walk together
   - Include adult supervision
   - Establish safe routes

#### Time Management Solutions
1. **Flexible School Scheduling**
   - Work with school to accommodate community needs
   - Consider evening or weekend classes
   - Adjust schedule during planting/harvest seasons

2. **Chore Sharing**
   - Create a system for sharing household responsibilities
   - Rotate chores among families
   - Provide support for single parents

3. **Community Childcare**
   - Organize community childcare for younger children
   - Allows older siblings (especially girls) to attend school
   - Can be run by rotating parents

#### Social Change Solutions
1. **Awareness Campaigns**
   - Educate community on the importance of education
   - Highlight success stories
   - Address gender norms

2. **Parent Support Groups**
   - Regular meetings for parents to share experiences
   - Peer support and encouragement
   - Advocacy for education

3. **Mentorship Program**
   - Pair successful adults with youth
   - Provide guidance and support
   - Share experiences and advice

## Group Activity: SWOT Analysis

### Strengths
- Strong community cohesion
- Parents are committed to their children's education
- Existing community structures (PTA, women's groups)
- Willingness to work together
- Some successful examples of community support

### Weaknesses
- Limited financial resources
- High illiteracy rates among parents
- Traditional gender roles
- Limited awareness of available support
- Some families are isolated

### Opportunities
- Government education programs
- NGO support for education
- Community's willingness to contribute
- Youth motivation and engagement
- Potential for income generation

### Threats
- Economic downturn
- Political instability
- Climate change affecting agriculture
- Increasing school fees
- Brain drain (educated youth leaving community)

## Parents' Commitments

During the session, parents made the following commitments:

1. **Regular Attendance**: Ensure their children attend school regularly
2. **Support Learning**: Help children with homework and study at home
3. **Community Participation**: Actively participate in community education initiatives
4. **Resource Contribution**: Contribute to community scholarship fund as able
5. **Advocacy**: Advocate for better education services and infrastructure

## Observations

### Positive Observations
- Parents are highly committed to their children's education
- Strong sense of community and willingness to work together
- Parents have practical ideas for solutions
- Parents are willing to make sacrifices for their children's education

### Concerns
- Financial constraints are severe
- Gender disparities are deeply ingrained
- Some parents have limited education themselves
- There's a sense of resignation among some parents

### Surprising Insights
- Parents are more aware of the value of education than expected
- Parents are willing to challenge traditional norms
- Parents have strong networks and support each other
- Some parents are already implementing creative solutions
